F12|BMJ/PubMed, 1996|1|ok|There's also an older but still relevant finding worth mentioning: among the most heavily dependent smokers, specifically those who crave a cigarette the moment they wake up, 4mg gum was found to be the most effective form of replacement therapy, helping around one in three of them quit (BMJ/PubMed, 1996).
F4|Scientific Reports, 2022|1|ok|A randomized crossover trial comparing a 4mg nicotine pouch to 4mg nicotine gum found the pouch produced a peak blood nicotine level roughly twice as high as the gum, about 8.5 ng/mL versus 4.4 ng/mL, and delivered roughly double the total nicotine exposure over the same period (Scientific Reports, 2022).
F7|PubMed/Cochrane systematic review, 2025|1|ok|A 2025 systematic review of randomized trials on tobacco-free nicotine pouches found that while they appear well tolerated and may reduce how many cigarettes people smoke, there's currently no statistically significant evidence that they increase actual quit rates compared to gum, snus, or even placebo (PubMed/Cochrane systematic review, 2025).
F14|Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, Hartmann-Boyce et al., 2025|1|ok|A separate Cochrane review from the same year went further, stating that the evidence is simply uncertain whether oral nicotine pouches help people quit smoking at all compared to just telling someone to keep smoking as usual (Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, Hartmann-Boyce et al., 2025).
F8|Addiction, Ottawa Heart Institute, 2024|1|ok|Research out of Addiction journal, done with the Ottawa Heart Institute, backs this up: pouches may help some people cut down on cigarettes, but there's no evidence they outperform the nicotine replacement therapies that have been proven in large trials and used in clinical care for decades (Addiction, Ottawa Heart Institute, 2024).
