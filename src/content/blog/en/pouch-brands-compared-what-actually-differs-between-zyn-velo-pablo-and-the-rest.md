> ZYN, Velo, Pablo: what really differs between nicotine pouch brands, and why the differences matter less than you think when you're trying to quit.

You're standing in front of a gas station shelf full of tins: ZYN, Velo, Pablo, On!, a dozen others that all look roughly the same from three feet away. Comparing pouch brands feels like it should matter, stronger flavor, lower nicotine, a "cleaner" formula. But once you strip away the branding, the differences between ZYN, Velo, Pablo and the rest are smaller, and less useful to you, than the packaging wants you to believe.

If you're new to pouches altogether, it's worth first reading [what nicotine pouches actually are and whether they're safer than cigarettes](/blog/what-are-nicotine-pouches-and-are-they-safer-than-cigarettes). This article assumes you already know the basics and want to know what separates one tin from the next.

## Is ZYN different from Velo when it comes to nicotine?

Brands advertise differences in nicotine source, moisture level, and pouch material as if they were meaningful health distinctions. Most of that specific product-level detail, exact nicotine milligrams, whether the nicotine is plant-derived or synthetic, how "wet" or "dry" a pouch feels, comes from the brands' own marketing pages and retailer comparison charts, not from independent research. That doesn't make it false, but it does mean you should treat precise brand-by-brand claims the same way you'd treat a sales pitch: interesting, not proof.

What's actually been studied is the category as a whole, not one tin versus another. The FDA has found that nicotine pouch products contain lower levels of most harmful and potentially harmful constituents (HPHCs) compared to other oral and smokeless tobacco products. That's a statement about pouches as a category next to snus or chewing tobacco, not about ZYN being cleaner than Velo or Pablo being worse than On!.

## Are some pouch brands healthier than others?

Here's where it gets more useful. A 2026 review in *Internal and Emergency Medicine* found that chemical analyses and biomarker studies consistently place nicotine pouches at the lowest end of the toxicant risk continuum, with harmful constituents like tobacco-specific nitrosamines largely undetectable or present at negligible levels compared to snus and combustible cigarettes. That's a strong finding, but again, it applies to pouches as a class of product. Nobody has published independent, peer-reviewed data showing that one major brand carries meaningfully less risk than another.

So when a friend tells you Pablo "hits harder" or ZYN is "the cleanest one," what they're describing is mostly nicotine strength labeling and personal sensation, not a documented safety gap. ## Why does switching brands not make quitting easier?

This is the part that actually matters if you're trying to cut down or stop. A huge number of people who try to quit end up brand-hopping instead: switching from a 6mg ZYN to a lighter Velo, or from Pablo down to an On! mini, hoping the change in strength or flavor will make the craving smaller. It rarely works for long, because withdrawal isn't tied to a brand. It's tied to nicotine leaving your bloodstream.

According to UC Health, nicotine withdrawal produces irritability, poor concentration, restlessness, depressed mood, anxiety, increased appetite, and craving, regardless of what product delivered the nicotine in the first place. Your brain doesn't know or care whether the tin said ZYN or Pablo on the label. It only knows the nicotine level in your blood is dropping, and it wants that fixed.

That's why swapping brands so often turns into a stall tactic rather than progress. You feel like you're doing something different, but the underlying dependence hasn't moved. ## Are nicotine pouches a legitimate quitting tool?

One more thing worth being blunt about: nicotine pouches are not approved by the FDA as nicotine replacement products used to help quit smoking, according to reporting from NBC News based on FDA data. People sometimes use them that way informally, swapping cigarettes for pouches and calling it quitting, but that's not what they were designed or authorized for. If your actual goal is to get off nicotine entirely rather than just change delivery format, treating pouch brand choice as a health strategy is the wrong frame from the start.

So the honest answer to "what's the difference between ZYN, Velo, Pablo and the rest" is: less than the shelf display suggests, and less than it matters. Flavor, tin design, and marketed strength vary. The addiction underneath doesn't. If you've been circling different brands looking for the one that finally makes this easier, that search is worth redirecting toward quitting itself.
