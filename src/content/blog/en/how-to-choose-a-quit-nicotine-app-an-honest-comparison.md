Search "best app to quit nicotine" and you'll find dozens of lists.

Almost all of them are written by one of the apps in the ranking. That's not automatically dishonest. But that app rarely ends up below first place.

This one is different. NiQuit sits lower down, described the same way as everyone else. No attempt to come out on top.

How this was checked: each app's own store listing and website, in September 2026. What it actually covers, what it costs. What it feels like to use day to day isn't included, since that's not fair to judge for an app we don't use ourselves. Store listings change, so check for yourself before deciding.

## What actually varies

Feature lists are a bad way to choose. A craving tracker, a money-saved calculator, a health timeline, almost every app in this category has those now. Not a difference. A baseline.

Three things actually differ.

**What nicotine it covers.**

Velm and Quit Pouches: pouches and snus only.

This is Quitting: vaping, aimed at teens.

QuitNic, Puff Pacer, Quitty, and NiQuit: cigarettes, vaping, and pouches together.

Why that matters: people switch products mid-quit constantly. Quit cigarettes, moved to vaping. [Dependence runs on the same receptors](/blog/nicotine-addiction-is-not-about-the-product-its-about-the-molecule) no matter which product you pick up. Same molecule. Same part of the brain. The dependence didn't go anywhere, it just changed packaging.

An app that only tracks one product can't see that switch. A craving logged under vaping in one app and a craving logged under cigarettes in another look like two small, separate problems. They're really one continuous one.

**How it structures the quit.**

Kwit: gamification. Levels, unlockable content, badges.

Smoke Free: data. Detailed statistics, minimal coaching.

QuitNic: AI coaching layered on top of tracking.

NiQuit: a course of short lessons plus an AI coach for cravings in the moment. Closer to a program than a tracker.

**How you pay for it.**

A subscription behind basic tracking is common in this category. NiQuit doesn't have one. Course, coach, tracking, all free. The app runs on donations.

This isn't a knock on subscriptions. Building any of these costs real money, and a subscription is a fair way to cover that. Just know it going in, ideally not three weeks into a quit, right when you hit a paywall on the one feature you'd started relying on.

## The apps, plainly

**Kwit.** Smoking and vaping. Heavy on gamification and community. Good fit if achievement systems and streaks keep you going.

**Smoke Free.** Smoking and vaping. Detailed stats, light on coaching. Good fit if you want data over encouragement.

**QuitNic.** Cigarettes, vaping, pouches. AI coaching on top of tracking. Closest to NiQuit in scope.

**Velm, Quit Pouches, and QuitNicPouches.** Pouches and snus only. Good fit if that's your only nicotine source.

**NiQuit.** Cigarettes, vaping, pouches, and more, together. A course plus an AI coach. Free, donation-funded, no subscription.

None of this makes one app objectively the best. It just makes some a better fit for some people.

## How to actually pick

Only use one nicotine product? Get the simplest tool for that exact product, Velm for pouches, for example. A general app will feel like overkill.

Moved between products, cigarettes to vaping, vaping to pouches, and back? Get a general tracker. It treats that as one addiction instead of three separate counters, so you're not restarting your streak every time you switch.

Respond to game mechanics, look at Kwit. Want raw data over encouragement, look at Smoke Free. Want a structured program, lesson by lesson, that's NiQuit.

Try the free tier of two or three before settling. The feature list doesn't decide this. Whether you still open it next week does.
