Search "best app to quit nicotine" and you'll find dozens of ranked lists. Almost all of them are written by one of the apps in the ranking. That's not necessarily dishonest, but it does mean the app writing the list is rarely ranked lower than first. This one is different in one specific way: NiQuit is included below, described the same way as everyone else, with no attempt to come out on top.

For this list: nicotine-source coverage and pricing model were checked directly against each app's own store listing and website in September 2026. What's not included is any claim about how it feels to use a competitor's app day to day, since that's not something we can evaluate fairly for an app we don't use ourselves. App store listings change often, so check an app's own page before assuming this list is current.

## What actually varies between these apps

Most comparison lists lead with feature counts, which is a poor way to choose, because almost every app in this category now has a craving tracker, a money-saved calculator, and some kind of health-recovery timeline. Those are table stakes, not differentiators. Three things actually differ.

**What nicotine it covers.** Some apps are built for one product only: Velm and Quit Pouches focus specifically on nicotine pouches and snus, This is Quitting focuses on vaping for teens and young adults. Others, including QuitNic, Puff Pacer, Quitty, and NiQuit, track cigarettes, vaping, and pouches together, on the reasoning that switching products mid-quit is common and worth tracking as one habit, not three.

The reasoning for tracking multiple products together isn't just convenience. Nicotine dependence [runs on the same receptors](/blog/nicotine-addiction-is-not-about-the-product-its-about-the-molecule) regardless of delivery method: a cigarette, a vape, and a pouch all deliver the same molecule to the same part of the brain. Someone who quits cigarettes by switching to vaping hasn't necessarily broken the dependence, they've changed its packaging. Apps that only track one product can't see that switch happening; a craving logged against "vaping" and a craving logged against "cigarettes" in two separate apps look like two smaller problems instead of one continuous one.

**How it structures the quit itself.** Kwit leans into gamification: levels, unlockable content, achievement badges. Smoke Free leans into data: detailed craving-pattern statistics with comparatively little coaching. QuitNic adds AI-based coaching on top of tracking. NiQuit is built around a fixed course, a sequence of short lessons, plus an AI coach for in-the-moment cravings, closer to a structured program than a pure tracker.

**How you pay for it.** Subscription paywalls behind core tracking features are common in this category. NiQuit doesn't have one: the course, coach, and tracking are free, and the app is funded by optional donations rather than a subscription. This isn't a knock on subscriptions themselves, building and maintaining any of these apps costs real money, and a subscription is a legitimate way to fund that. It's simply a fact worth knowing before you're three weeks into a quit and hit a paywall on the one feature you'd started relying on.

## The apps, described plainly

**Kwit** - smoking and vaping, heavy on gamification and community groups. Good fit if achievement systems and streaks keep you motivated.

**Smoke Free** - smoking and vaping, detailed statistics with minimal coaching. Good fit if you want data more than encouragement.

**QuitNic** - cigarettes, vaping, and pouches, with AI coaching layered on tracking. Closest in scope to NiQuit.

**Velm, Quit Pouches, and QuitNicPouches** - pouches and snus specifically, no smoking or vaping tracking. Good fit if pouches are your only nicotine source and you want a tool built around that one habit.

**NiQuit** - cigarettes, vaping, pouches, and other nicotine sources together, structured as a course plus an AI coach, free with optional donations, no subscription.

None of these facts make one app objectively "the best." They make some apps a better fit for some people.

## How to actually pick

If you only use one nicotine product and want the simplest possible tool, a specialized app for that exact product, Velm for pouches, for example, will feel less cluttered than a general one built to cover everything.

If you've moved between products, cigarettes to vaping, vaping to pouches, and back, a general tracker that treats it as one addiction instead of three separate trackers will save you from restarting your streak every time you switch.

If you respond to game mechanics, look at Kwit specifically. If you want raw data over encouragement, look at Smoke Free. If you want a structured program you follow lesson by lesson rather than a tracker you check in on when you remember, that's the corner NiQuit sits in.

Try the free tier of two or three before settling on one. The thing that actually predicts whether a quit-nicotine app helps you isn't its feature list. It's whether you still open it next week.
